## 1. The answer, first

On the synthetic portfolio (**$33.85M wholesale a year**), four measured leaks cost **$5.61M a year, 16.6% of revenue**. The solution is modelled to recover **$335K to $1.00M a year (mid $669K), 1.0% to 3.0% of revenue**.

For a toy manufacturer with **$500M** of wholesale revenue and the same loss profile, that is **$4.95M to $14.84M a year (mid $9.89M)**, against a measured run cost of about **$8K a year** at list price at this scale. Retiring the incumbent AutoML licence is extra and is not in these numbers (the customer supplies it).

And today, before any of that: the app shows **$1.38M of revenue at risk in the next 13 weeks, 434 critical positions ($510K) that run out within two weeks**, ranked, with a recommended split for each.

### Why each capture rate, and how the dollars are valued

* **Recover sales lost to stockouts.** Forecast error is 29% lower than the naive forecast (measured). The high case assumes lost sales fall in proportion; low and mid assume a third and two thirds of that. Lost sales are valued at gross margin (38%, an assumption), not at revenue: the margin is what the business loses.
* **Plan inbound on ocean instead of expediting by air.** Share of today's air-expedited units that a 13-week forecast flags early enough to move by ocean (9-16 week lead time). Assumed; the earlier the spike is visible, the higher. Only the premium is saved: air costs 7.0x ocean per unit (measured), so moving a unit saves 86% of its air cost.
* **Buy and allocate less into a cooling licence.** Conservative: markdown is driven mostly by when a licence collapses, which a demand forecast sees only partly. Measured loss is already margin forgone, so no margin factor.
* **Fewer compliance chargebacks.** Share of late or short PO lines avoided by allocating constrained supply to commitments before the PO week rather than in the meeting after it.

## 6. What would make these numbers wrong

* **The loss profile is synthetic.** The real business may leak more or less than 16.6% of revenue. Discovery step one is to replace the four measured losses with the customer's own (lost sales, air freight, markdown, chargebacks).
* **The forecast is weakest where the loss is.** On trend-driven items the high-case forecast covers 74% of actual demand against a 90% target, so exposure there is understated and the high case is optimistic for those items.
* **Capture rates are assumptions**, set conservatively and shown as a range. They should be measured in a pilot: one licence family, one account, 8-12 weeks, decisions captured in the app against a hold-out set planned the old way.
* **Gross margin (38%) is an assumption.** Lost sales scale linearly with it.
* **Not counted:** planner time saved, shelf facings kept at the next line review, and the incumbent AutoML licence. All are real, none is measured here.
